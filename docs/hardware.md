# Hardware

## Reference kit

| Item | Purpose | Approximate cost |
|---|---|---|
| Clip-on 200× lens with LED ring (e.g. APEXEL 200X) | magnification and reflected light | see retailer |
| Phone stand, copy stand or small tripod with phone holder | removes hand shake, fixes working distance | low |
| Stage micrometer, 1 mm in 100 divisions (10 µm) | calibration | low |
| Clear nail polish and clear adhesive tape, glass slides | epidermal impressions | low |
| White LED panel, tablet screen or second phone torch | transmitted light for impressions | usually already available |

Any recent phone with a main camera of 12 MP or more should work. Record the phone model in every calibration profile, because magnification depends on the phone's camera module.

## Notes on the clip-on lens

Reviews of the APEXEL 200X report that it is very sensitive to camera shake at full magnification and that the ring LED is not very bright ([PetaPixel review](https://petapixel.com/2023/03/04/testing-the-apexel-200x-led-lens-a-microscope-for-your-phone), [Digital Camera World review](https://digitalcameraworld.com/reviews/apexel-200x-phone-microscope-lens-review)). A stand is therefore part of the kit, not an accessory. A listing for a related model (APL-MS200) gives a clip range of 71 to 105 mm, 12 LEDs and a UV mode ([listing](https://www.skroutz.eu/s/67754746/apl-ms200-phonemicro-5-phone-camera-lens-macro-microscope-filter-200x.html)); check which variant you have.

No published field-of-view or resolution figure for the standalone 200X lens was found. Both are measured during calibration (field area in mm², smallest resolved micrometer spacing) and recorded in the profile notes.

## Clamping and repeatability

The magnification changes if the lens shifts relative to the camera window or if the phone switches camera modules. To keep it stable:

1. Mount the clip so the lens is centred on the main (1×) camera. Mark the clip position on the phone case with a fine marker so it can be re-mounted the same way.
2. Disable automatic lens switching (often called auto macro or macro control) so the phone does not change to the ultra-wide camera when the subject is close.
3. Use a fixed zoom setting (1× or a set digital zoom) and record it in the profile.
4. Re-photograph the micrometer every time the clip is re-mounted, or at least at the start of each session. The validation protocol measures how much µm/px varies between re-mounts; if that variation is small, a per-session check is enough.

## Illumination modes

The lens's LED ring gives reflected light. This works for looking at the leaf surface directly, but contrast on a flat, waxy or hairy epidermis can be poor.

Epidermal impressions (nail polish peels on a slide) are usually easier to image with transmitted light: switch off the ring LED, put the slide on a white LED panel or tablet screen, and place the clip-on lens on top. The validation protocol compares both modes for each species.
